# Temporal Meta-index Research - 2026-08-30

Status: exploratory. This note does not change the published methodology or
release schema.

## Objective

Build a temporal index that represents a domain's stable current strength while
distinguishing persistent directional movement from random volatility. A
one-week score spike should not cause an immediate promotion, while sustained
growth supported by multiple observations should not be penalized as noise.

## Data

- 13 homogeneous weekly snapshots from `data-2026-W23` through
  `data-2026-W35` (`2026-06-07` to `2026-08-30`).
- All snapshots use methodology `v0.3.0-beta` and the same public schema.
- W4 and W8 candidates are compared on the common evaluation interval from
  `2026-07-26` through `2026-08-30`: five week-to-week transitions.
- A domain must be scored in the current snapshot and in at least 75% of the
  candidate history window.

The public archives expose only `consensus_score`, not source-specific
percentiles. This is an intentional licensing boundary rather than missing
publication functionality.

## Architecture Decision

Continue with a meta-index over existing public release fields. Do not publish
or retain separate source ranks or percentiles as temporal artifacts. The
weekly BigQuery build remains stateless and continues to replace raw and mart
tables after producing the aggregate release.

The working Dynamic Domain Reputation Index (DDRI) is therefore an operational
temporal reputation measure, not a source-aware latent factor and not a
calibrated probability that a domain is safe or truthful. Its four components
are constrained to:

| Component | Available evidence |
| --- | --- |
| `reputation_score` | Temporal level of public `consensus_score` |
| `reputation_confidence` | History length, observation continuity, coverage/source-count stability, and temporal residual noise |
| `reputation_trend` | Confidence-filtered trend of aggregate `consensus_score` |
| `observed_risk` | Published risk flags, source count, and threat types; absence of a flag is not evidence of safety |

This keeps the public licensing surface unchanged and allows every result to be
reproduced from existing weekly release archives.

## Findings So Far

1. Raw week-to-week score volatility mixes directional movement and noise. On
   the 723,590 domains scored in every one of the latest eight snapshots, the
   correlation between raw score standard deviation and absolute OLS slope was
   `0.8378`.
2. A rolling mean provides most of the available stability improvement. W4 SMA
   reduced median p95 index drift from `9.7483` to `3.2287`; W8 SMA reduced it
   to `1.8124`.
3. Penalizing raw rolling standard deviation is not attractive. The penalty
   itself changes each week and can increase index drift while also penalizing
   smooth trends.
4. Direct OLS fitted-level and half-week momentum adjustments are too
   aggressive, especially near the compressed top of the score distribution.
   W8 direct momentum retained only `77.20%` of the latest raw top-1k and `1%`
   of the latest raw top-100.
5. Residual standard deviation has the right interpretation for trend quality,
   but adding it to an aggressive OLS fitted level did not beat EWMA in the
   current sample.
6. Small confidence-filtered momentum is much safer than direct momentum.
   Rank-band caps allow more movement below the top-1k without applying the
   same score adjustment throughout the ranking.
7. A one-time diagnostic on the three temporarily available source-level
   snapshots showed that aggregate score movement is much larger when the set
   of ranking sources changes. This can be detected from the already-published
   `ranking_sources_present` field without retaining source values.

## Ephemeral Source-level Diagnostic

The source-aware idea was evaluated once using `2026-08-15`, `2026-08-23`, and
`2026-08-30` mart states. Queries returned aggregate counts only; source-level
rows were not exported or retained. Movement above `0.5` score points was
classified as up/down, and an individual source was considered aligned when it
moved in the same direction by more than `0.1` score points.

| Transition | Direction | Source set | Domains | Mean absolute score change | Two or more aligned | Aligned majority |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2026-08-15 -> 2026-08-23 | up | unchanged | 188,271 | 3.1920 | 32.42% | 12.38% |
| 2026-08-15 -> 2026-08-23 | up | changed | 32,512 | 7.3906 | 8.10% | 1.50% |
| 2026-08-15 -> 2026-08-23 | down | unchanged | 209,300 | 3.2040 | 39.06% | 15.28% |
| 2026-08-15 -> 2026-08-23 | down | changed | 40,136 | 9.1885 | 10.04% | 2.11% |
| 2026-08-23 -> 2026-08-30 | up | unchanged | 168,364 | 2.8017 | 30.52% | 11.52% |
| 2026-08-23 -> 2026-08-30 | up | changed | 29,800 | 8.3773 | 7.05% | 1.23% |
| 2026-08-23 -> 2026-08-30 | down | unchanged | 180,453 | 2.7748 | 32.67% | 12.73% |
| 2026-08-23 -> 2026-08-30 | down | changed | 22,579 | 7.5234 | 8.85% | 1.76% |

The same pattern appears in both transitions: changing source composition is
associated with roughly 2.4-3 times larger score movement and very weak source
agreement. Treat a change in `ranking_sources_present` as a structural shock:
reduce reputation confidence and require subsequent aggregate observations
before interpreting the movement as trend.

The low aligned-majority shares for unchanged source sets should not be used to
calibrate a source-agreement threshold. Several inputs update at different
cadences, and two transitions are insufficient for source-level modeling.

## Candidate Definitions

The current experiments use an EWMA level plus an optional confirmed trend.
For an OLS slope `b` and its standard error `SE(b)`:

```text
confirmed_slope = sign(b) * max(abs(b) - 1.5 * SE(b), 0)
```

The two global variants add `0.1 * confirmed_slope` or
`0.25 * confirmed_slope` to EWMA.

The scale-aware variant normalizes confirmed slope by the robust within-band
slope scale and applies `tanh` saturation. Its maximum score adjustments are:

| Current raw rank | Maximum adjustment |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1-100 | 0.01 |
| 101-1,000 | 0.03 |
| 1,001-10,000 | 0.10 |
| 10,001-100,000 | 0.30 |
| below 100,000 | 0.75 |

The asymmetric variant multiplies confirmed downward adjustments by `1.5`.
These caps are experimental values, not selected methodology parameters.

domains in the `2026-08-30` release. They are intentionally not combined into

| below-100k | 772,412 | 67.38 | 77.45 | 24.39% | 25.16% | 16.28% | 34.16% | 27.78% |

Overall reputation-score p10/p50/p90 is `51.58/69.61/88.96`; heuristic
confidence p10/p50/p90 is `59.53/78.00/92.20`. Confidence is the geometric
mean of W8 history completeness, mean source coverage, source-set continuity,
and rank-band-normalized temporal-noise confidence. It is descriptive and not
a calibrated probability.

Trend requires both statistical evidence (1.5 slope standard errors) and
practical materiality (at least 10% of the current rank-band adjustment cap).
Without the materiality condition, microscopic but low-noise score changes
incorrectly classified most top-100 domains as directional.

Observed risk remains sparse: 870,857 domains (`99.82%`) have no currently or
recently observed public risk signal, 1,237 have one current risk source, 211
have recent-history-only risk, and 107 have two or more current risk sources.
`none-observed` must not be interpreted as evidence of safety.

## Aggregate-only DDRI Candidate Test

The component snapshot was followed by a walk-forward test of candidate final
scores. All candidates use only public aggregate fields. The common W8 sample
contains five transitions; consequently, these results can reject ineffective
ideas but cannot select production coefficients.

- `level`: W4 EWMA reputation level.
- `trend-gated`: level plus confidence times the scale-aware W8 trend.
- `shock-gated`: hold the previous W4 EWMA and suppress trend for one week when
  `ranking_sources_present` changes.
- `shock-damped-k`: accept fraction `k` of the new EWMA movement and suppress
  trend on a source-set-change week.
- `conservative-k`: shock-gated score minus `k * rank_band_cap *
  (1 - confidence)`.

`shock-damped-0.5` is the only extension that currently earns a place in the
shortlist. Compared with plain level, overall p95 drift is effectively
unchanged (`3.9144` versus `3.9125`), while p95 movement during a structural
shock falls from `7.1187` to `4.2017`. Full shock gating minimizes the median
shock movement but has a worse tail and greater overall lag.

The trend adjustment is directionally correct but adds almost no ranking
benefit on this sample and slightly worsens retention and drift. Keep trend as
an exposed DDRI component and label for now rather than forcing it into the
score. Direct confidence penalties also failed to improve any decision metric;
use confidence as metadata and as a gating signal, not as a generic subtraction
from reputation. Observed risk remains a separate layer rather than a score
penalty.

## Implementation Decision

Publish the components and `shock-damped-0.5` candidate from this repository as
a separate experimental archive. Do not add columns to the primary consensus
CSV. A dedicated workflow runs after a successful weekly consensus refresh,
reads the latest eight public releases, and attaches the DDRI files to the same
weekly release and to `data-latest`.

The production identifier is `ddri-v0.1.0-experimental`. Parameters remain
frozen during the observation period; any formula change requires a DDRI
methodology version change. Failure of the experimental build does not block or
invalidate the primary consensus archive.

A production-path dry run for `data-2026-W35` emitted 872,412 rows in a 20 MB
compressed archive. It recorded 723,590 domains with complete W8 history and
54,561 current structural shocks. Component distributions matched the earlier
exploratory snapshot.

## Parameter Strategy

The available history is too short for a full Cartesian parameter search. Tune
one component at a time and retain a Pareto set instead of reducing all metrics
to one arbitrary weighted loss.

1. Select the level estimator: W4/W6/W8 and EWMA half-life 2/3/4 weeks.
2. With the level fixed, test confidence thresholds `kappa = 1.0/1.5/2.0`.
3. Test global momentum coefficients `0.05/0.1/0.25` as controls.
4. Test rank-band cap multipliers `0.5/1.0/1.5` around the current cap schedule.
5. Test downward asymmetry `1.0/1.5/2.0` only after a stable cap schedule is
   selected.
6. Add aggregate residual-volatility and one-week shock penalties last.

Evaluate every stage separately at top-100, top-1k, top-10k, top-100k, and
below top-100k. Required diagnostics include retention, rank displacement,
response lag to persistent trends, false promotion after a one-week spike, and
behavior when source coverage changes.

## Required Observation Period

- **13 snapshots (current):** enough for feasibility checks and rejecting
  unstable formulas; not enough for coefficient selection.
- **26 homogeneous snapshots:** minimum for a preliminary model choice. A W8
  model then has 18 walk-forward transitions. Starting at W23, this threshold
  is reached at `data-2026-W48`, expected around `2026-11-29`.
- **52 homogeneous snapshots:** preferred for production calibration and for
  observing seasonal and source-refresh regimes. Starting at W23, this is
  reached around `2027-05-30`.

Calendar length alone is insufficient. Before treating parameters as stable,
the evaluation window should also contain source-coverage changes, several
large positive and negative domain movements, and at least a few one-week
spikes followed by reversals.

## Deferred Source-aware Design

A source-aware dynamic factor model was considered as a stronger way to
separate domain movement from individual-source noise:

```text
latent reputation: q[t] = q[t-1] + trend[t-1] + process_noise
source observation: x[source,t] = bias[source] + loading[source] * q[t] + noise[source,t]
conservative index: E(q[t] | data) - lambda * sqrt(Var(q[t] | data))
```

Such a model could react quickly when several sources move together and reject
a single-source spike. It requires historical source-level observations,
however. Publishing or retaining those observations was rejected because the
project intentionally avoids redistribution of source-specific rankings and
derived percentiles for licensing reasons.

Keep this design as a documented alternative only. Reconsider it if licensing
terms or an approved non-persistent evaluation mechanism changes; it is not a
dependency for the aggregate DDRI path.
